# GHC Study — 2013 original firearms markings, proof marks and ammunition audit
Date: 15 October 2026

## Primary evidence actually checked

**Original source paper:** connected Drive [`Practice Hunting Test  2013-12-19.pdf`](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1f_bWpni_W7xpReNHPHCWittNiQSBFOwX/view), `PART III` Questions 7, 14, 15, 16 and the actual printed `Answer Key`, `Section 3` corresponding rows: **Q7 B, Q14 B, Q15 A, Q16 A**. Original PDF text directly fetched and inspected. The historical key has not been edited.

**Actual GHC 2026 classroom deck:** connected Drive `Weapons - Ammo (20260214).pdf` (Drive file ID `1aO-ftzIXRw6acqMYlpb71ZG7nV8hZ1Mp`), readable text directly checked, sections `Firearms Markings`, `Ammunition, Centerfire vs Rimmed`, `Ammunition, Rimmed vs Rimless or Rimfire`, `Ammunition, Shotguns`. Original slide teaches manufacturer, serial number, **type of ammunition (not only caliber)**, barrel/chamber details and proof marks; centerfire vs rimfire primer location; `R` suffix means rimmed brass (not rimfire); shotgun gauge is conventionally counted by lead spheres per pound, unlike .410 expressed as inches. Classroom slide says **`Firearms without Proof Marks are illegal!`**; that sweeping classroom slogan is **not** a complete summary of current statutory exemptions in BeschG §4, so the app does not adopt it without qualification.

**Current official federal legal text:** independently reviewed [WaffG §24(1)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/waffg_2002/__24.html) mandatory identifying details on regulated essential parts, [BeschG §3](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/beschg/__3.html) official proof-testing baseline for introducing firearms to the German market, [BeschG §4](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/beschg/__4.html) categorical/temporary exemptions and foreign proof-mark **mutual recognition**, and [BeschG §6](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/beschg/__6.html) marking successful official proof testing. Regulations have exceptions and transitional circumstances. WaffG identification and BeschG proof-marking have different legal purposes.

## Four directly compared 2013 questions

| ID | Original exam key | Finding and classification |
|---|---|---|
| `P2013-S3-Q07`, “Which cartridge is a RIMFIRE?” | **B (.22 LR)** | **Source qualified:** .22 LR uses a rim-initiated primer, while .222 Remington and rimmed **7x57R** are centerfire. `R` designates a projecting case rim, not where the primer sits. 2026 GHC `Centerfire/Rimfire` sections directly checked. |
| `P2013-S3-Q14`, “Legal markings of a rifle?” | **B** | **Source qualified with current-law scope:** historical choice says serial, proof house, maker, **ammo type**, matching class slide. WaffG §24 has further required manufacture/import identifiers and exceptions; BeschG §6 independently regulates proof marks. The 2013 four-part answer is **not exhaustive** for all current marking cases. |
| `P2013-S3-Q15`, “US made guns must be inspected by a German Beschussamt after import.” | **A / True** | **Overbroad historical-law claim, explicitly flagged:** BeschG §3 establishes a proof-testing baseline, but §4 recognizes certain **foreign proof marks under mutual agreements** and several exceptions. U.S. manufacturer origin alone does **not** unambiguously establish the exact proof-testing action or whether the firearm falls under an exemption; a typical U.S. factory safety test is not automatically equivalent to a government-recognized proof mark. Original True remains an **archival answer only**. A nonspecific **pre-answer warning** explains the possible law difference without revealing the keyed response. |
| `P2013-S3-Q16`, “Which shotgun barrel has the bigger diameter?” | **A / 12 gauge** | **Source qualified:** 12-gauge nominal bore is larger than 20-gauge; fewer lead balls needed for one pound implies each sphere is larger. Nominal gauge and chamber length (`12/70`, `20/70`) are separate; .410 is designated differently. |
